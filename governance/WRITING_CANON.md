# Writing standard

Write under these rules from the first sentence. Preserve the user's point, useful detail and personal voice while making the text easy to read.

Lead with the concrete outcome or the decision needed. Put structure on the page (a short list, a small table, numbered steps) rather than a wall of text, and give one decision at a time with its context. Write times for people in the reader's local time, the way people say them. Use active verbs and familiar words. Connect each fact to its implication when that helps the reader. Distinguish observed, implemented, tested, deployed and unknown states. Cite the source for a material claim without inventing evidence.

Remove empty introductions, generic praise, importance claims, canned conclusions, rhetorical questions answered immediately, and repeated contrasts written for rhythm. Avoid jargon where the reader needs a plain explanation. Explain internal identifiers when they matter; omit them when they do not.

Choose punctuation for its purpose. Prefer periods, commas, parentheses or a colon over habitual em dashes. Lists are for genuinely parallel items; headings are for useful structure. Neither replaces connected reasoning.

Read the complete draft, preserve its factual meaning, and make the minimum effective edit. Check factual accuracy, clarity, relevance, voice and repetition. Do not smooth a distinctive, useful detail into a generic claim.

Every prose surface follows the standard: chat, handoffs, code-review descriptions, operating rules and external copy. Where writing is judged by others, the three skeptics in `HARNESS_CORE.md` section 8 judge it; a cut for leanness names the waste, and shorter or nicer wording alone is not waste. A writing rule remains Told unless an actual enforced mechanism and its coverage are demonstrated.
